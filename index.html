1. Introduction: Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets)

A Generative Flow Network (GFlowNet) is a probabilistic framework for constructing complex objects by sequentially sampling trajectories in a directed acyclic graph (DAG). Each trajectory corresponds to a sequence of actions that produces a complete object (for example, a finished Tetris board or a fully formed molecule). A GFlowNet assigns non-negative flow (probability mass) to each trajectory so that the marginal probability of sampling any complete object is proportional to a user-defined reward for that object.

Unlike conventional reinforcement learning, which typically converges to a single best policy, GFlowNets aim to learn a distribution over many high-reward outcomes. This property of proportional sampling is especially valuable in applications where multiple viable solutions are required, such as diverse move sequences in games or candidate molecules in drug discovery.

The figure contrasts the behavior of a standard single-path reinforcement learner with that of a GFlowNet. In the traditional RL approach (left), the policy concentrates probability mass along one “best” trajectory. In contrast, the GFlowNet (right) spreads its flow across several promising paths. Each path in the diagram represents an alternative construction strategy. By maintaining multiple plausible routes, GFlowNets preserve exploration and remain robust if the optimal solution changes over time.

Implementation Details

Training begins with a large batch of self-play episodes in a custom Tetris environment. Each episode records the full sequence of piece placements (the “trajectory”) along with the final game score. Initially, the network imitates a strong expert heuristic to provide stable behavior. Once the imitation phase is complete, training switches to the Trajectory Balance objective.

The Trajectory Balance loss enforces that the total flow assigned by the network to a trajectory matches the exponentiated reward of its final state. Formally, for a trajectory \(x_0 \to x_1 \to \cdots \to x_T\) with reward \(R(x_T)\), model flows \(F(x_t \to x_{t+1})\) and per-piece normalizers \(\log Z\), the loss is

$$\mathcal{L}_{TB} \;=\;\bigl(\log Z \;+\;\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log F(x_t \to x_{t+1})\;-\;\log R(x_T)\bigr)^{2}$$

Intuitively, this trains the network so that the product of predicted flows along a trajectory (times the normalizer) equals the true reward. During backpropagation, both the per-action flows and the normalization constants \(\log Z\) are adjusted to satisfy this equality in expectation over sampled trajectories.

The core model is a deep convolutional network (a ResNet-style trunk enriched with Squeeze-and-Excite blocks) that extracts spatial features from the board. These features, plus one-hot encodings of the current and next pieces, feed into an MLP head that outputs unnormalized log-flow scores for every rotation and horizontal position. Gradients are clipped to prevent instability and the learning rate decays in steps as training progresses. The key metrics like average score, loss curves, learning rate and the normalization constants (“log Z”) for each piece type are logged to TensorBoard throughout.

After training concludes, the final network weights and learned normalizers are exported for client-side inference. In the browser, these parameters power an ONNX runtime that computes move flows in real time without any server calls.

Heuristic and State-Space Reduction

Although the GFlowNet can in principle score every legal placement, the number of terminal moves can grow very large. To keep the demo responsive, a parallel heuristic computes board features such as aggregate column heights, hole counts and depths, row transitions, bumpiness and a non-linear bonus for line clears and performs a one- or two-step lookahead.

At inference, the raw log-flows from the GFlowNet and the lookahead heuristic scores are each scaled to [0,1] and blended (e.g., 50/50 mix). This hybrid score ranks all candidate placements. To avoid redundant options, placements leading to identical resulting boards are merged, keeping only the highest-scoring version. Finally, the top three unique moves are shown in the sidebar and highlighted in the DAG. This approach preserves the GFlowNet’s diversity while focusing on the most promising moves for fast, interactive play.

3. Core Concepts & Flow Conservation

The principles demonstrated in the Tetris demo extend naturally to molecular generation. In this context, each node represents a partial molecule and each edge an added fragment.

In molecular design, a GFlowNet sequentially adds fragments to an evolving structure until a complete molecule is produced and scored by a reward function. Thanks to flow conservation, the total incoming probability at each intermediate molecule is redistributed among its possible extensions. Higher-reward molecules therefore attract more flow, while alternative structures retain non-zero probability and remain available for sampling.

The interactive visualization below lets you adjust the reward function using a slider. As you move the slider, the percentage of generated molecules shifts in real time. Clicking the “Maximize weight” button sets the reward to favor heavy molecules. Under this setting, the heaviest molecule appears about 49% of the time, whereas lighter molecules receive minimal flow.

Technical Insight: Flow = Reward at Terminal Nodes

In a GFlowNet, every path through the DAG from the root to a terminal node carries a flow value. At convergence, the total flow arriving at a terminal node \(x_T\) exactly equals the reward \(R(x_T)\) assigned to that complete molecule. Formally, if all flow contributions along each trajectory multiply (or sum in log-space) to the reward, then sampling from the GFlowNet produces molecules with frequencies proportional to their desirability.

During training, the network adjusts its per-action flows and normalization constants so that:

$$\sum_{\text{paths}\,\to\,x_T} F(\text{path}) \;=\; R(x_T)$$

This property guarantees that the fraction of samples corresponding to any molecule matches its share of the total reward mass, ensuring faithful proportional sampling of high-quality candidates.

Pretraining GFlowNets for Fast Inference

GFlowNets can be pretrained by investing compute time upfront to fit flows across many example trajectories. Once training completes, the model can generate diverse high-reward molecules almost instantly, without further search.

In practice, a pretrained GFlowNet produces large batches of candidate molecules with a single forward pass, dramatically accelerating the exploration phase in drug discovery. This contrasts with traditional search or optimization methods, which often require expensive iterative evaluations for each new molecule.
